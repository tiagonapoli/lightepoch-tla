LSE CASAL vs LDAXR/STLXR announce: codegen hazard and mitigations

Mechanism suggested by the model
--------------------------------
CASAL is modeled as one acquire-release RMW operation. Its acquire annotation orders later operations after the atomic operation as a whole, and its release annotation orders earlier operations before it. In contrast, the LDAXR/STLXR sequence is two separate instructions: LDAXR acquire orders later operations after the exclusive load, and STLXR release orders earlier operations before the exclusive store, but that pair does not compose into the same StoreLoad-style edge from the successful store to the later objectUnlinked load. The herd results reflect exactly that distinction: direct LSE CASAL forbids the outcome, but the acquire/release exclusive loop permits it unless an explicit post-store barrier is added.

Practical interpretation
------------------------
The model-level cheapest robust mitigation among these rows is not a pre-barrier; it is a post-announce full barrier. Either the exclusive-loop CAS needs a DMB ISH after the successful STLXR, or the announce can be modeled as plain STR followed by DMB ISH. The latter is immune to LSE-vs-exclusive-loop CAS codegen differences in this litmus.

Refresh-side codegen check
--------------------------
The refresh-side result depends on an ordinary acquire load of currentEpoch:

  LDAR currentEpoch; STR readerSlot; LDR objectUnlinked

LDAR is a base AArch64 load-acquire instruction, not an LSE atomic RMW. There is no LSE-vs-LL/SC lowering choice analogous to CASAL for that instruction in the litmus. Therefore the codegen variability found for the CAS announce does not apply to the refresh-side LDAR result, assuming the implementation really requests/receives an acquire load rather than a plain LDR.

    STORE localCurrentEpoch = 0     Interlocked.Increment(waiterCount)  <- full-fence RMW
    LOAD  waiterCount               CAS on localCurrentEpoch (claim)
    if (waiterCount > 0) signal     if the CAS fails -> semaphore.Wait()

If the releaser reads waiterCount as a stale 0 while the waiter's claim CAS reads
localCurrentEpoch as a stale non-zero, nobody signals and the waiter sleeps
forever on a slot that is in fact free. The audit graded this as an unconditional
hang on the grounds that an acquire load cannot close store buffering.

That reasoning is correct for the abstract acquire of the .NET memory model and
WRONG for the instruction AArch64 emits, because AArch64 acquire/release are
RCsc: an LDAR may not be reordered before an earlier STLR, so STLR;LDAR supplies
StoreLoad ordering on its own. LDAPR is RCpc and drops exactly that edge.

| STLR ; LDAR (CONTROL) | LDADD (relaxed) | lightepoch_release_wakeup_waiter_relaxed.litmus | Sometimes 3 7 (allowed) |

The last row is the sensitivity control and it is what makes the three Never
verdicts worth anything: weakening the waiter's RMW to a relaxed LDADD does
produce the hang, so the litmus can express the outcome and the forbidden
verdicts are real forbiddance rather than a malformed test.

Conclusion: the existence of this hang does not depend on the algorithm. It
depends only on whether the JIT emits ldar or ldapr for the volatile read of
waiterCount. Neoverse N2 implements FEAT_LRCPC, so the JIT is permitted to choose
ldapr. Pending disassembly on ARM64 hardware. Note that at the .NET memory model
level the concern stands regardless, since the spec promises only acquire and
does not promise RCsc.
